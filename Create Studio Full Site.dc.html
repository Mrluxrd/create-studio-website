Overview

Property first. Everything else steps back.

Luxury buyers judge a property in seconds, and they judge the platform showing it just as fast. Luxury Listings DR needed a presentation equal to the inventory it carries.

The structure lets visitors move between destinations and individual properties without ever losing the thread back to an inquiry. The project is currently in development.

What made it difficult

Real estate platforms accumulate weight quickly: photography, categories, locations, filters and competing calls to action. Left unmanaged, that abundance reads as noise rather than choice.

The brief asked for an elevated presentation that still gets someone from browsing to enquiry without friction.

How we worked

Destinations became the organising principle. From there, each property gets a focused page, responsive layouts and one obvious action rather than five competing ones.

Imagery leads. The interface stays quiet around it, and the content hierarchy does the explaining.

The solution

Built for discovery, aimed at enquiry.

A destination-led structure and a deliberately restrained interface keep attention where it belongs — on the property.

Destination-led property discovery
Responsive property presentation
Clear navigation
Focused property information
Inquiry-oriented calls to action
A premium visual system suited to luxury real estate
A structure prepared for continued portfolio growth

Authority that is easy to read.

Legal expertise is only persuasive if a prospective client can find it. The Joan Leonardo site needed to carry the weight of the practice while being genuinely simple to move through.

Trust in legal work is established in the first few seconds. Dense blocks of text, unclear service grouping and dated interface conventions all undercut it before a visitor reads a word of substance.

A sharper hierarchy was needed — without trading away the gravity a practice of this kind should project.

Structure first, then a more confident visual language on top of it. Typography carries most of the authority; the layout does the rest.

Overview

Designed around the work, not the software.

A luxury property sale is a long, many-sided conversation. Advisors juggle buyers, sellers, listings, developers, appointments, follow-ups, documents, referrals and transactions across cycles that run for months.

This project scopes a CRM built for that reality rather than around it. It is currently in development.

What made it difficult

Off-the-shelf platforms ask the business to bend. Fields get repurposed, workflows get approximated, and eventually the real process moves back into spreadsheets and messages.

The work began by describing precisely how this advisor manages relationships, live opportunities, property information and next actions — then designing to that description.

How we worked

The workflow was mapped before a single screen was drawn. From it came a set of connected modules covering:

The problem

Presentable is not the same as persuasive.

Most business sites are assembled from a template and filled with whatever content already existed. They look acceptable and perform poorly: visitors cannot find what they came for, the mobile experience arrives second, and no page offers an obvious next step.

Our solution

Structure earns the right to be styled.

Work starts from the commercial goal and the decision the visitor has to make. Page hierarchy is settled before visual exploration begins, and the build follows the approved design exactly — responsive, accessible, quick, and configured so your team can edit content.

Visual design that has aged, content bolted on a page at a time, a mobile experience nobody ever really designed, and pages slow enough to lose the visitor before the first sentence.
